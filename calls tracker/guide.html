Basics

  • Filter at the top of the page: Priority list, one or more call lists by their assigned_list (e.g. 12Nov-D4). It starts on the lists being called today (from their first to their last call day, e.g. 12Nov-D4 on 16–18 Nov); clear it to see all lists. Supervisor review, To be revised and Attendance ignore it.
  • Refresh now re-reads the Google Sheet. Otherwise the data refreshes by itself every 5 minutes.
  • Who is counted. Only people recruited as Eligible – signed up. Refusals and ineligibles at recruitment cannot be called, so they are left out of every figure.
  • "Now". Overdue and "due in the next hour" are measured against the latest submission in the data, not the clock. During field hours the two are minutes apart.

Call-list order

Each recruitment day's sign-ups are split into two call lists by assigned_list (4 or 5), and call_order is each pid's position in its list, from the randomisation. First attempts follow this order strictly, and so does the next-attempt pile: the day-4 list before the day-5 list, each in call_order. Both columns come from the data entry sheet.

Shift windows

shifts_to_try lists the windows nobody has called in yet, or says All tried / None tried. Spread calls across windows: someone who never answers in the morning may answer in the evening.

What is final

  • A completion is final. Calls logged after it are ignored, and flagged on the Calls summary.
  • A supervisor's closure is final. A call logged after it does not reopen the case. A completion still beats an earlier closure.
  • An enumerator's closure is not final until the supervisor validates it. Until then, a new call reopens the case.

🗓️ Attendance

For one day (today by default): who was planned, who checked in, and which list they actually called. It reads the check-in Sheet: the schedule tab (the plan) and the data tab (the start- and end-of-day form). The filter at the top does not apply here.

  • Tiles: one per planned shift, checked in out of expected (green = everyone, orange = some, red = nobody, grey = not started yet), and how many people are calling a list other than their own.
  • Assignment: the plan for the day by shift and list, each person marked ✅ checked in, ⏳ not checked in or 🟠 calling another list, to follow up. Each call list has its own colour, the same in this table and in Everyone today, with a colour key above.
  • Everyone today: one row per person who was planned, checked in or made a call that day: planned list and shift, then the start-of-day form (arrival, lateness, tablet, top-up) and the end-of-day form (time left, device, top-up, balance) side by side, even when the two forms spelled the name differently, then calls made, how many were on their planned list (on_list) and how many on other lists (other_list, with which lists).
  • Flags: 🔴 planned but no check-in (or calling without one) · 🟠 calls on another list · 🔵 not on the schedule · 🟡 more than 15 minutes late · ⚪ name not in the enumerators tab (add the spelling to also_typed_as).
  • Calls on another list are listed call by call under the table. A callback, a case sent back from Supervisor review or a pid you assigned can be a good reason, so check before acting.
  • The list of a call is the assigned_list of the pid called. To change someone's list, change the schedule tab; the dashboard reads it again within 5 minutes.

✅ Active pids — the work queues

Every open pid sits in one queue. Work through them from the top. Each table starts with assigned_list (the day-4 or day-5 call list), then the pid. If the supervisor has left a note on a pid, it appears as supervisor_note right after the pid: read it before calling.

🔁 To be revised — Data Team checked

Cases the supervisor put back to work in Supervisor review (status ACTIVE, or reopened). Call these first, and read supervisor_note: it says why and what to do. The first call afterwards moves the pid back into its normal queue; while it is listed here it does not appear in the other queues.

✔ done: once the supervisor has taken the action in the note, tick the case, choose who did it under Done by and click Mark as done. It is saved in the decisions log and the case leaves this list, so it does not look like new work: it goes back to its usual queue (with the note) until someone calls it. Ticked cases are listed, with who and when, under ✔ Done below the table, and no longer count in To fix. A tick from before a later reopen does not count.

🆕 To be assigned

Eligible sign-ups nobody has called yet. Hand them out strictly by call_order: the top row is always the next pid to give out. Never skip ahead (a 5 before a 3), and never repeat a case while new ones are waiting. days_waiting counts the days since recruitment.

One list at a time: choose the call list under Assign from list (each shows how many are left). It starts on a list being called today. Each list is handed out on its own, in its own call_order.

1skipped: should already have been tried
  • Skipped (red rows): each call list (one recruitment day's day-4 or day-5 list) has a front, the highest call_order already tried. A pid more than 8 places behind it (one round of 8 enumerators) was passed over. Example: the team is on #20–28 and #8 has never been called. The skipped column says how far the list has got. Hand these out first.

Assigning in the dashboard

  1. Tick the pids you are handing out, lowest call_order first.
  2. Choose the enumerator for each in assign to.
  3. Choose your name in Assigned by (it goes into the log; the list is everyone marked supervisor = 1 on the enumerators tab), then click Assign.

Each assignment is recorded in the decisions tab of the Google Sheet: when (Sierra Leone time), who assigned it, the pid and the enumerator. Assigned pids leave this list. Only first calls are assigned here.

Out of order: if you tick a pid while a lower number in the same list is still waiting, you are skipping: the dashboard says so in red, names the pid(s) being skipped, and asks for a reason before it saves. The reason goes into the log. Assignments in order need no comment.

📨 Assigned, not called yet

Pids assigned in the dashboard that nobody has called yet, longest waiting first, with who they were given to and how many minutes ago. Red after 30 minutes: each enumerator takes one case at a time, so a case should be called well within that.

↩ Undo an assignment: open it, tick the assignments to take back, choose who is undoing it (and why, if useful) and click Undo. Only pids nobody has called yet are listed. They go back to 🆕 To be assigned; the undo is kept in the decisions log.

📵 To keep calling

Codes 2_IN 2_NA 2_OF 2_WN

No answer or phone off, or a wrong number or wrong person with other numbers still to try. The list starts with the fewest attempts; within the same number of attempts, it follows the call-list order (assigned_list, then call_order). Take the top row.

121 attempt only

After call_order come the shift windows not tried yet (shifts_to_try) and whether the respondent ever picked up (ever_picked_up). Then the totals: all calls (total_attempts) and how many ended in no answer (times_noanswer), phone off (times_off), a reschedule (times_rescheduled) or a drop (times_dropped). Someone who has picked up before but keeps rescheduling needs a different approach from someone whose phone is always off.

The last three columns describe the last call: last_callcode, last_enumerator and last_comment.

numbers_wrong is the most numbers any single call found wrong. The form counts per call, not in total, so this never counts the same wrong number twice.

✅ Completed shows the interview and its quality: list, enumerator, submission time, whether it was ever partial-saved, the effort it took (attempts, wrong numbers, reschedules, drops, no answer, phone off), the top-up sent and how, the last comment, the survey-quality questions (qc1_noise to qc6_difficult_other, blank until they are on the form) and full_end_survey / partial_end_survey.

Refusal (0_R) shows the pid, list, enumerator and submission time, whether the refusal came on the notification form (notification_form), why they refused (refuse_why, or rs_refuse_why on the notification form), the consent answer (c01), the attempts and the effort totals before the refusal, and the last comment.

🧩 Partially completed, right below it, lists the partial-saved interviews (was_partialsaved = 1) that the Data Team decided to close (a supervisor closure, not a complete), and the completes whose form ended as a partial (partial_end_survey = 1), which are taken out of ✅ Completed. They appear here instead of under their usual section: the last section reached and why it stopped, the top-up, the quality questions and the end-of-survey answers. Partial saves that are still open stay in ✅ Active pids and 💾 Partial saves.

Reopen a final closure

For closures that are already final: validated by the Data Team, or closed by the Data Team. Closures not validated yet are not listed here; send those back from To validate with status ACTIVE. Completed interviews cannot be reopened. Choose the pid, write the reason (shown to the enumerators) and click Reopen. The case goes back to the state of its last open call and appears first in Active pids, under To be revised — Data Team checked. If it is closed again later, that closure needs validating again.

Reviewing one pid

  1. Pick the pid (click its row in the list, or choose it in the Pid box). Why it needs validating names its group, then its history: attempts, last real call, reschedules, drops, shifts not yet tried, partial saved.
  2. Open All observations to see every submission. Grey rows are Communications form submissions (timing or status only, never interview answers); blue rows are earlier dashboard entries.
  3. Choose the action, the code if needed, write the comment, and save.

Every save adds one row to the decisions tab of the Google Sheet. Nothing is overwritten: changing your mind means a new entry, and the newest one for a pid counts. Do not edit that tab by hand. The Log shows every entry, newest first.

Counts shown in the flag examples are illustrations, not current figures.
